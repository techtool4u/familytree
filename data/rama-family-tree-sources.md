### Rama's paternal line (Bala Kanda 1.70)

Brahma, Marichi, Kashyapa, Vivasvan (Surya), Vaivasvata Manu, Ikshvaku, Kukshi, Vikukshi, Bana, Anaranya, Prithu, Trishanku, Dhundhumara, Yuvanashva, Mandhata, Susandhi, Dhruvasandhi (brother Prasenajit), Bharata, Asita, Sagara, Asamanja, Amshuman, Dilipa, Bhagiratha, Kakutstha, Raghu, Pravriddha (Kalmashapada), Shankhana, Sudarshana, Agnivarna, Shighraga, Maru, Prashushruka, Ambarisha, Nahusha, Yayati, Nabhaga, Aja, Dasharatha, **Rama**.

### Sita's line (Bala Kanda 1.71)

Nimi, Mithi, Janaka I, Udavasu, Nandivardhana, Suketu, Devarata, Brihadratha, Mahavira, Sudhriti, Dhrishtaketu, Haryashva, Maru, Pratindhaka, Kirtiratha, Devamidha, Vibudha, Mahidhraka, Kirtirata, Maharoma, Swarnaroma, Hrasvaroma, then **Janaka** (Sita's father) and Kushadhvaja.

## Variants and caveats

1. **Valmiki vs. Kalidasa and the Puranas.** In Valmiki, Raghu comes many generations before Rama, and Aja is the son of Nabhaga. In Kalidasa's Raghuvamsha (and in Puranic lists), the order is Dilipa → Raghu → Aja → Dasharatha. The Puranas, such as the Bhagavata Purana (9th canto), also give a much longer list of kings. This tree follows Valmiki and notes the variant on Aja's profile.
2. **Yayati.** Bala Kanda 1.70.42 has Nahusha → Yayati → Nabhaga, but Ayodhya Kanda 2.110.32 has Nahusha → Nabhaga. Yayati is included and tagged *Variant*.
3. **Sagara's mother.** Asita had two pregnant queens, and one (Kalindi) poisoned the other. Translations disagree on which queen bore Sagara, so his mother is left blank.
4. **Sita.** She was found in the earth, not born to Janaka. She is linked to Janaka and Sunayana as her adoptive parents and tagged *Adopted*.
5. **Shanta.** She is included as Dasharatha and Kausalya's daughter because that is the popular tradition. Scholars (Goldman; Chatterjee) regard this as a later northern addition, so she is tagged *Disputed*.
6. **No dates.** These are sacred and mythological figures with no historical dates, so birth and death years are left empty. OurFamilyTree's Timeline and Map views therefore stay empty for this tree. The Tree, Fan, Wheel, filters and Relationship finder all work.
7. **Uttara Kanda details** (the brothers' sons and their cities) were checked through Wikipedia, which in turn cites translations such as H. P. Shastri's and the 1894 Chackravarti edition. For citation-grade work, check them against a printed critical edition: the Baroda Critical Edition, or Goldman et al., *The Rāmāyaṇa of Vālmīki* (Princeton).
